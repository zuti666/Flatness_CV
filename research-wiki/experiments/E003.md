# Interpretation

## Finding 1: Near-Orthogonal Gradient Subspaces
`cos_flat_fisher ≈ −0.02 to −0.04` across all tasks. Near-zero (not strongly negative → not adversarial; not positive → not redundant). The flatness component and Fisher component operate in **nearly orthogonal subspaces** of the gradient space.

## Finding 2: Extreme Scale Separation
Fisher gradient norm is only 0.014%–0.286% of the clean gradient. The flatness (GAM) component is 3.4–6.7× the clean gradient. This means:
- AS^(1) completely dominates the primary gradient direction
- Fisher applies a micro-correction in a subspace orthogonal to flatness

## Finding 3: cos_clean_flat Stable at +0.65
The GAM/flatness gradient is consistently well-aligned with the clean task gradient (+0.635 to +0.699). This confirms AS^(1) is not causing gradient conflict with the primary task objective.

## Finding 4: Fisher Drift Grows Monotonically
`normalized_fisher_drift` grows from 4.8e-6 (task 1) to 7.1e-5 (task 9), showing Fisher penalty is accumulating and actively constraining drift into old-task sensitive directions.
