---
id: C5
title: SAM effect is trajectory-level, not merely task-local
type: empirical
status: supported
---

Applying SAM in later tasks improves retention of earlier tasks, even when those earlier tasks were trained with SGD. The effect is not limited to the task where SAM is applied.

**Evidence (Exp D, CIFAR10 2-task)**:
- Applying SAM only at Task 2 (sgd_sam_factor): improves old-task retention A_{1,2} by +0.36 vs SGD_SGD.
- Applying SAM only at Task 1 (sam_factor_sgd): improves A_{1,2} by only +0.06.
- Random-factor does not reproduce this asymmetry.

**Evidence (Exp F, ImageNet-R r16 T20, forked trajectory)**:
- From same SGD task-9 checkpoint: suffix SAM-factor gives FAA 66.38 vs 61.72 for suffix SGD.
- Prefix forgetting: 11.03 vs 17.19.
- sgd_random_factor ≈ sgd_sgd (FAA 61.72, forget 15.50), proving the benefit is not from noise.

**Strongest evidence**: Exp F is the most controlled — same-checkpoint fork ensures prefix is identical; only suffix optimizer differs.

**Theoretical implication**: Supports Theorem B.1 (pathwise decomposition) — sharpness at task i bounds R_i(Q_t) for t > i, meaning later SAM-optimized posteriors are more compatible with earlier task solutions.
