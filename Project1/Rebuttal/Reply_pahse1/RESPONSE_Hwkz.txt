Thank you for your review. We address each concern directly.

W1 (Motivation)

We agree that SAM applied to a frozen-backbone LoRA model naturally perturbs only the trainable adapter parameters. However, this does not resolve which perturbation scope is correct in LoRA-based models — two published works explicitly disagree: LoRA-SAM (NeurIPS 2024) uses adapter-only perturbation; Flat-LoRA (ICML 2025) argues this is insufficient and advocates full-parameter perturbation including the frozen backbone. This is an unresolved inconsistency in how flatness is defined for LoRA models.

This inconsistency becomes structural in PECL, where updates are restricted to the task-permissible subspace due to the frozen backbone. Perturbations over the full parameter space extend beyond the admissible update directions, creating a mismatch between the perturbation domain and the optimization domain. Our contribution is a formal resolution: Theorem 4.4 shows the bound-relevant sharpness is exclusively within W_{Delta,t}. Table 2 confirms empirically: full-scope degrades accuracy (DeltaAcc^cls = -2.00 to -6.84 across all LoRA variants) while adapter-scope improves it (+0.05 to +0.86).

W2 (Missing related work: PAC-Bayesian CL analysis)

On Pentina & Lampert (ICML 2014) [already cited in our paper, Sections 1 and 2]:
P&L derives a bound on the multi-task risk assuming i.i.d. tasks from a fixed time-invariant hyperdistribution — task ordering is irrelevant and shuffling leaves the bound unchanged. This is multi-task learning, not sequential CL. As our paper states and Figure 2 illustrates: "in continual learning, the prior used at step t is typically produced by a history-dependent rule that progressively incorporates information from earlier tasks, rather than being generated from a single time-invariant hyperdistribution (Multi-task Learning)." Our framework captures this via a filtration-adapted hyperposterior {P_t} with drift penalty KL(P_t||P_{t-1}). P&L is a strict special case of Theorem 4.1 when drift = 0 (Section 4.1).

On Friedman & Meir (ICLR 2026) — concurrent independent work:
Three structural differences: (i) Prior hierarchy: F&M uses P_t = Q_{t-1} (flat chain, no distribution over distributions); we introduce P_{t-1} in M(M(H)), a genuine two-level hierarchy. (ii) Bound target: F&M bounds only cumulative plasticity loss — their Corollary 3.1 is self-described as "a straightforward extension requiring no new technical tools"; we bound the trajectory-averaged test risk capturing both stability and plasticity, with drift KL(P_t||P_{t-1}) as an explicit stability measure. (iii) No sharpness or subspace structure in F&M; Theorem 4.4 reduces the bound to the frozen-backbone adapter subspace (Lemma 4.2+4.3), absent from F&M entirely. We will add an explicit comparison to Related Work.

W3 (Additional PEFT approaches)

Beyond SeqLoRA, IncLoRA, and OLoRA already in the paper, we are adding results for InfLoRA and SD-LoRA — two additional LoRA-based PECL methods with different subspace management strategies. This directly addresses the request for broader PEFT validation. The framework targets LoRA specifically because its low-rank subspace structure makes the subspace reduction in Theorem 4.4 precise; extension to other PEFT families (prefix tuning, adapter modules) is possible when a well-defined W_{Delta,t} exists, and we will discuss this in the revision.

W4 (Undefined notation H)

H denotes the hypothesis class (predictors f_W: X->Y). It appears before its formal definition in Section 3.1 — we will add an inline definition at first use (page 2, line 101).

Supplementary experiments include: ViT-B/16 on five fine-grained benchmarks (CUB200, Cars196, Flowers102, FGVC-Aircraft, OxfordPet) plus a cross-domain CL scenario; T5-small/T5-large on NLP CL benchmarks; and two additional LoRA methods (InfLoRA, SD-LoRA). We believe the formal reframing of motivation and the structural differentiation from P&L and F&M address the main concerns raised.
