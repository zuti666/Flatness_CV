Revisiting Sharpness in Low-rank Subspaces for Continual Learning
Liying Wang, Hong Qiuhe, Yuyang Liu, Dipam Goswami, Simone Magistri, Xuanshuo Fu, Mengda Ji, Andrew D. Bagdanov, Joost van de Weijer  
09 Jan 2026 (modified: 09 Feb 2026)ICML 2026 Conference SubmissionConference, Senior Area Chairs, Area Chairs, Reviewers, AuthorsRevisionsCC BY 4.0
Verify Author List: I have double-checked the author list and understand that additions and removals will not be allowed after the abstract submission deadline.
Abstract: 
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) enables parameter-efficient continual learning (PECL) for large pre-trained models by confining task updates to a low-rank, task-permissible subspace.
While sharpness-aware optimization improves generalization in full-model learning, its role under subspace constrained updates remains unclear. This prompts the question of whether controlling subspace sharpness (sharpness within the task-permissible subspace) suffices to improve generalization across tasks, and how it relates to the sharpness of the full model.
In this paper we define the subspace sharpness and derive a sequential hierarchical PAC-Bayesian bound that augments empirical risk with a subspace sharpness term and a hierarchical KL term for cross-task posterior complexity. We compare subspace-restricted and full-parameter perturbation scopes and evaluate multiple sharpness types within the task-permissible subspace.
Curvature diagnostics further show that lower subspace sharpness is associated with higher continual accuracy and less forgetting across tasks.


Primary Area: General Machine Learning->Transfer, Multitask and Meta-learning
Keywords: Continual Learning；LoRA; PAC-Bayesian Theory;

Summary: 
This paper studies how task-subspace sharpness and full-parameter sharpness affect generalization in PECL, and investigates the use of SAM to improve PECL performance. The authors define subspace sharpness and derive a sequential hierarchical PAC-Bayes bound for this setting. Experiments are conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of optimizing sharpness within the task subspace.


Strengths And Weaknesses: 
Pros:
This paper provides an in-depth analysis of subspace sharpness and offers several insights. For example, it derives a PAC-Bayes bound tailored to the PECL setting. In addition, the authors support their claims with experiments demonstrating the effectiveness of subspace SAM.


Cons:  


    

  1. I am not convinced that practitioners would use full-parameter perturbation when applying SAM to LoRA. To my knowledge, this corresponds to a specific and somewhat unnatural implementation, since frozen parameters typically do not produce gradients. Therefore, the practical importance of the problem studied in this paper appears limited, which is the main weakness of the work.  
    2. 

  2. The PAC-Bayes bound largely builds on prior work, and I did not see major technical challenges in the derivation.  
    3. 

  3. The paper does not introduce a new method, but mainly validates the effectiveness of combining LoRA with different variants of SAM.  
    4. 

  4. Experiments are conducted only in a single setting (ViT on ImageNet), and the scope and scale of evaluation are relatively limited.
    5.

Originality: 2: fair

Summary: 
This paper studies sharpness-aware optimization in the context of parameter-efficient continual learning (PECL) with LoRA adapters. The authors derive a sequential hierarchical PAC-Bayesian bound showing that both the sharpness penalty and KL complexity terms reduce to quantities defined on the task-permissible LoRA subspace. They further argue that perturbations should be restricted to this subspace, and that first-order adversarial sharpness (AS(1)) most effectively suppresses dominant curvature directions within it. Experiments on ImageNet-R, ImageNet-C, and ImageNet-P support these claims across three LoRA organizational strategies.


Strengths And Weaknesses: 
Soundness. The theoretical framework is generally well-motivated. Lemma 4.2's translation-invariance argument is sound, and the KL decomposition onto the subspace is clean. The experiments are also reasonable and show convincing results.
Presentation. The paper is clearly written and the progression from theory to experiments is logical. The paper is also appropriately referenced. However, the three sharpness types (RS(0), AS(0), AS(1)) are introduced purely through equations, and the paper would benefit from an intuitive figure illustrating their geometric differences - particularly since the performance ordering maps directly onto the sharpness strength ordering, which is the paper's central empirical finding. Figure 4 is useful but arrives late and is too technical to serve this purpose.
Significance. The scope mismatch finding - that perturbing frozen backbone parameters hurts rather than helps - is a practically important and non-obvious result for practitioners using SAM-style methods with LoRA. The consistent advantage of AS(1) across all settings is also a useful empirical finding.
Originality. The extension of hierarchical PAC-Bayes to a sequentially evolving hyperposterior with an explicit drift penalty seems like a genuine contribution. The combination of this framework with LoRA-specific structure has not been done before to the reviewer's knowledge.


Soundness: 3: good
Presentation: 4: excellent
Significance: 3: good
Originality: 3: good
Key Questions For Authors: 
Theorem 4.1: The classical bound in Theorem 3.1 contains a log⁡m\log m logm term in the numerator inside the square root, which is absent in Theorem 4.1. Where does this term go in the sequential proof? Please provide a detailed accounting of this step in the proof sketch or appendix.
Lemma 4.2: The lemma assumes QtΔ≪PtΔQ_t^\Delta \ll P_t^\Delta QtΔ​≪PtΔ​, but implicitly also requires the prior to be supported on the same subspace WΔ,t\mathcal{W}_{\Delta,t} WΔ,t​ as the posterior. For IncLoRA and OLoRA, where subspaces grow or are constrained across tasks, is this assumption actually satisfied? If not, how does this affect the KL decomposition?
Bound tightness: PAC-Bayes bounds are often too loose to be numerically meaningful. Are the authors able to evaluate how tight Theorem 4.4 is in any of their experimental settings, or is the bound intended primarily as a conceptual motivation for the empirical investigation?


Limitations: 
no major limitations


Overall Recommendation: 5: Accept: Technically solid paper, with high impact on at least one sub-area of AI or moderate-to-high impact on more than one area of AI, with good-to-excellent evaluation, resources, reproducibility, and no unaddressed ethical considerations.

Summary: 
This paper explores the interaction between sharpness-aware optimization and low-rank subspace constraints (e.g., LoRA) in Parameter Efficient Continuous Learning (PECL). The authors note that traditional sharpness-aware methods perturb the entire parameter space, creating a disconnect with PECL's dynamics, which updates only within task-specific subspaces. To address this, the paper introduces the concept of “Subspace Sharpness” and derives a novel sequential hierarchical PAC-Bayesian generalization bound. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that controlling sharpness within the subspace suffices to provide generalization guarantees when the backbone network is frozen. Experiments validate that applying first-order sharpness minimization (AS-1) within task-permissible subspaces significantly enhances continuous learning accuracy and mitigates forgetting across multiple LoRA variants (SeqLoRA, IncLoRA, OLORA).


Strengths And Weaknesses: 
Strengths:


    

  1. The paper proposes a sequential hierarchical PAC-Bayesian analysis framework that provides theoretical guarantees.
    2. 

  2. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks are conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
    3. 



Weaknesses:


    

  1. The motivation of the paper is not entirely convincing. Applying AS(1) perturbation only to the LoRA adapter appears to be the standard way of using SAM, and this practice does not seem to introduce the claimed perturbation misalignment issue. Moreover, the empirical results presented in the paper suggest that this default strategy is already the most effective.
    2. 

  2. The paper should discuss prior work that applies PAC-Bayesian analysis to continual learning, such as [1] and [2].
    3. 

  3. Additional PEFT approaches should be included to better validate the effectiveness of the proposed subspace-restricted updates.
    4. 

  4. Some notations are used before being defined. For example, H in Line 101 (Page 2) is not formally introduced.
    5. 



References:


[1] A PAC-Bayesian bound for Lifelong Learning. ICML 2014.


[2] PAC-Bayes bounds for cumulative loss in Continual Learning. ICLR 2026.
